# Vectors and Scalars

***

## Understanding the basics of how objects move through a one dimensional space and time.

If an object travels in a straight line at a constant speed and a distance $d$ in a given time $t$ — the velocity ($v$) can be calculated with the given equation:
$$
\text{Velocity}\space v = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}
$$
The distance ($d$) can be found by doing the inverse of the equation above:

$$
d = v \times t
$$
## Applying vectors and scalars

In the section above, I showed several units such as *velocity* and *displacement* which can have a direction and magnitude — therefore these units are called *vectors*. You can draw a Vector Diagram to display the direction of the unit. This can be applied to other units including *weight*, *friction* and *force* since they can have a direction. A scalar can not have a direction, and think of it like a property of an object like *mass* and *speed*. Scalars can only have a magnitude. 

| Example *of* Scalars | Examples *of* Vectors |
| -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Speed                | Velocity              |
| Mass                 | Weight                |
| Volume               | Friction              |
| Time                 | Momentum              |
| Pressure             | Acceleration          |
| Power                | Force                 |
|                      |                       |

## Using graphs in Physics

![[distance-time-graph.png]]
The graph above shows two objects traveling over distance (*d*) and time (*t*).  Between points A to B the first object is at a constant velocity and at points B to C the object is stationary. On the second object that has a curved shape (non-uniform velocity) on the graph the velocity of the object doesn't have a constant rate between two points — so to calculate the velocity at a single point in time you could draw a straight line to plot the gradient and then calculate the velocity. E and F is our gradient. $x$ and $y$ is the measurement of the delta speed and time of the gradient. 

$$
\text{Velocity at E \textemdash F} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta y}
$$
